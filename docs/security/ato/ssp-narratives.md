## AC: Access Control

### AC-1 Policy and Procedures

**Status:** Inherited · **Responsibility:** Site

The [[SITE: organisation]] develops, documents and disseminates the access control policy and procedures for [[SITE: system name]] and reviews them [[SITE: frequency]]. OpenTrack supplies the product-specific procedures the site can reference: the administrator guide (docs/guides/admin.md), the deployment hardening checklist (docs/security/hardening.md) and the control mapping (docs/security/stig-mapping.md).

*Site:* Write and approve the access control policy and procedures, name the official who manages them, and set the review frequency.

### AC-2 Account Management

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack manages its own accounts (email-identified, with one role each: viewer, track_manager or admin) through Settings -> Users, the opentrack user command and the /api/v1/auth API: admins create, change the role of, turn off, sign out everywhere, unlock and delete accounts, and every such change is written with the acting account to the decision log and the hash-chained audit record. Turning an account off ends its sessions and revokes its API tokens at once, and OpenTrack refuses any change that would leave no active admin. SAML users get an account (origin saml) at first sign-on with the role mapped from the identity provider, which stays authoritative for that role. The [[SITE: account manager]] approves requests, assigns account types and roles, and reviews accounts [[SITE: frequency]].

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#users; crates/ot-server/src/auth/api.rs; crates/ot-server/src/user_cli.rs; crates/ot-store/src/auth.rs; crates/ot-store/src/sqlite.rs (record_decision); docs/security/stig-mapping.md (AC-2)

*Site:* Define account types, approvers and conditions for group/role membership; review accounts [[SITE: frequency]]; keep local accounts to break-glass and service use; notify account managers of transfers and departures.

### AC-2(1) Automated System Account Management

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Account management is automated in the product: the Users panel, the /api/v1/auth/users API and the opentrack user command create, modify, disable, unlock and remove accounts, and SAML provisions accounts and sets their role from the identity provider at every sign-on. Inactive accounts are turned off automatically (AC-2(3)) and every action is recorded in the audit record (AC-2(4)).

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#users; docs/guides/admin.md#saml; crates/ot-server/src/auth/api.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/saml.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/maintenance.rs

### AC-2(2) Automated Temporary and Emergency Account Management

**Status:** Partially implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack has no expiry date for accounts, so temporary and emergency accounts are not removed or disabled automatically at the end of their authorised period. API tokens always expire (1 hour to 10 years, default 365 days), and any account not marked Never turn off is disabled automatically after 35 days without a sign-in; break-glass accounts marked Never turn off are exempt. The [[SITE: account manager]] turns off or deletes temporary and emergency accounts within [[SITE: time period]] by procedure.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-store/src/auth.rs (User, disable_inactive); crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#api-tokens; docs/guides/admin.md#inactive-accounts

*Site:* Define the lifetime of temporary and emergency accounts and disable them manually (Settings -> Users, Active off, or opentrack user) until the product supports account expiry.

### AC-2(3) Disable Accounts

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Accounts that have not signed in for 35 days are turned off automatically (a fixed value); the check runs at sign-in and every 10 minutes, ends the account's sessions, revokes its API tokens and writes an account_disabled audit row with reason inactivity. Accounts marked Never turn off (break-glass) are exempt. Admins disable accounts immediately when they are no longer associated with a user or violate policy.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs (DISABLE_INACTIVE_AFTER_DAYS); crates/ot-server/src/auth/maintenance.rs (disable_inactive); crates/ot-store/src/auth.rs (disable_inactive); docs/guides/admin.md#inactive-accounts

*Site:* Keep Never turn off to a minimum of sealed break-glass accounts; disable accounts of departed users within [[SITE: time period]].

### AC-2(4) Automated Audit Actions

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Account creation, modification (role, password reset), enabling, disabling (by an admin or for inactivity), unlocking, session revocation and removal are written to the append-only audit record with actor and time, and each row is also emitted as an audit.record log event over OpenTelemetry to the site's collector. The site's SIEM notifies [[SITE: personnel or roles]] of these events.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-store/src/sqlite.rs (record_decision); crates/ot-store/src/audit.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/maintenance.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#audit-record; docs/guides/admin.md#opentelemetry

*Site:* Route audit.record events to the SIEM and configure notifications of account events to [[SITE: personnel or roles]].

### AC-2(5) Inactivity Logout

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Browser sessions end after 15 minutes without user activity (10 minutes for admins) and after 24 hours regardless of use; these values are fixed. The page's own polling does not count as activity: the browser sends the user's idle time (x-ot-idle-ms) with every call and the server ends the session when it exceeds the limit.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs (IDLE_MINUTES, ADMIN_IDLE_MINUTES, SESSION_HOURS); crates/ot-server/src/auth/sessions.rs (alive); ui/src/auth/activity.ts; docs/guides/admin.md#session-limits

### AC-2(13) Disable Accounts for High-risk Individuals

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

An admin can turn off an account at once (Settings -> Users, Active off, or opentrack user disable), which ends all its sessions and revokes its API tokens immediately; Sign out everywhere ends sessions without disabling. For SAML accounts the identity provider can also refuse the user. The [[SITE: organisation]] decides when an individual poses a significant risk and directs the disablement within [[SITE: time period]].

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#disabling-accounts; docs/guides/admin.md#revoking-sessions; crates/ot-store/src/auth.rs (revoke_api_tokens)

*Site:* Define the process and time period for disabling accounts of high-risk individuals.

### AC-3 Access Enforcement

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Every /api/v1 route is gated on the server by one policy table: reads need viewer, track changes (tracks, groups, registry, correlation suggestions, decisions, history) need track_manager, every other change and the reads of accounts, sign-in settings, configuration export/import, probe and the audit record need admin, and any new write route defaults to admin. The role is read from the account as it is at each call, so a role change or deactivation takes effect at once, and a session that must change its password can call nothing else. Source secrets are masked for everyone but admins. OpenTrack does not filter tracks by user clearance (a deliberate decision): every signed-in user may see every track, and label enforcement is downstream.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs (need); crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (layer); docs/guides/admin.md#roles; docs/security/stig-mapping.md (AC-3, AC-16, F-2)

*Site:* Operate the system so that every user is authorised for the highest security label any source carries (no per-user clearance filtering in OpenTrack).

### AC-4 Information Flow Enforcement

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack ingests only the sources an admin configures and publishes only to the NATS subjects and TAK outputs an admin configures; each published track carries the source security label (highest classification, union of restrictions, intersection of releasability) so downstream systems can enforce flow. Transport to and from these systems runs over TLS with mutual TLS available. OpenTrack does not filter output by label or clearance; flows between security domains, network boundary enforcement and cross-domain solutions are the site's.

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#security-labels; docs/guides/admin.md#encryption-in-transit; docs/nats-output.md; docs/security/hardening.md (Encrypt every link); docs/security/stig-mapping.md (AC-16)

*Site:* Enforce information flow at the boundary [[SITE: boundary devices]]; keep plaintext and multicast TAK outputs off networks not accredited for the picture's classification.

### AC-5 Separation of Duties

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack separates duties with three roles: viewers read, track managers curate the picture, and admins configure the system, accounts and sign-in. Admins cannot alter or delete the audit record (append-only, hash-chained), and its exported copy goes to the site's SIEM, where reviewers separate from OpenTrack admins examine it. The [[SITE: organisation]] documents the duties and assigns roles accordingly.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#roles; crates/ot-store/src/audit.rs

*Site:* Document separated duties and assign roles; have audit review performed in the SIEM by personnel who are not OpenTrack admins.

### AC-6 Least Privilege

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack grants three cumulative roles and checks them on the server for every route; SAML gives at most track_manager unless Allow admin is turned on, and never signs in to a local account. The container runs as a non-root user with all Linux capabilities dropped, no-new-privileges and a read-only root file system. The site assigns each user the least role their duties need.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#roles; docs/guides/admin.md#saml; docker-compose.yml; Dockerfile; docs/security/stig-mapping.md (AC-6)

*Site:* Assign least roles; keep Allow admin off unless the identity provider should make admins.

### AC-6(1) Authorize Access to Security Functions

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Security functions (accounts, API tokens, sign-in and SAML settings, banners, sources and their secrets, plugins, configuration export/import, purge and the audit record) are reachable only with the admin role, enforced by the server-side policy table.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs (ADMIN_READS, need); docs/guides/admin.md#roles

*Site:* Designate the individuals authorised for the admin role.

### AC-6(2) Non-privileged Access for Nonsecurity Functions

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

An OpenTrack account has exactly one role, so an admin who also operates the picture can be given a separate track_manager or viewer account (a distinct email) for non-security work. The site requires admins to use that non-privileged account for non-security functions.

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#roles; docs/guides/admin.md#adding-accounts

*Site:* Issue admins a separate non-privileged account and require its use for non-security functions.

### AC-6(5) Privileged Accounts

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Only an admin can grant the admin role, SAML cannot grant it unless Allow admin is on, and every role change is audited. The [[SITE: organisation]] restricts admin accounts to [[SITE: personnel or roles]].

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#saml; docs/security/hardening.md (Sign-in)

*Site:* Restrict admin accounts to [[SITE: personnel or roles]].

### AC-6(7) Review of User Privileges

**Status:** Inherited · **Responsibility:** Site

The [[SITE: ISSO]] reviews user privileges [[SITE: frequency]] and reassigns or removes them as needed. OpenTrack helps with Settings -> Users (role, origin, last sign-in, state per account) and the audit record of every role change (GET /api/v1/audit?op=update_user).

*Site:* Review roles [[SITE: frequency]].

### AC-6(9) Log Use of Privileged Functions

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Every privileged change (accounts, tokens, sign-in settings, sources, schema, settings, plugins, purge) is written to the decision log and, in the same transaction, to the hash-chained audit record with the acting account; reading and verifying the audit record is itself audited (audit_export, audit_verify). Each row is exported over OpenTelemetry as an audit.record event.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-store/src/sqlite.rs (record_decision); crates/ot-server/src/audit_api.rs; crates/ot-store/src/audit.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#audit-record

### AC-6(10) Prohibit Non-privileged Users from Executing Privileged Functions

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

The server refuses (403) any call whose path needs a higher role than the caller's, whatever the user interface shows; a saml account's role cannot be changed through the API, and a session that must change its password can call nothing but the password and session endpoints.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (layer); crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#saml

### AC-7 Unsuccessful Logon Attempts

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Three failed password sign-ins within 15 minutes lock the account until an admin unlocks it (fixed values), from Settings -> Users, the API, or opentrack user unlock on the server when every admin is locked out. Every refusal gives the same answer and takes as long as a wrong password, and the real reason (unknown account, locked, turned off, bad password) goes to the audit record with the client address. Sign-in attempts are also rate limited per client address (5 at once, then one a second). SAML and client-certificate sign-ins are limited by the identity provider and the PKI.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs (LOCKOUT_FAILURES, LOCKOUT_WINDOW_MINUTES, LOCK_MINUTES); crates/ot-server/src/auth/api.rs (login, unlock_user); docs/guides/admin.md#account-lockout

### AC-8 System Use Notification

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack provides a notice-and-consent banner (Settings -> Banners, up to 20,000 characters) that every user must accept at each sign-in before any access. The server keeps the acceptance and refuses every API call except the sign-in calls until it has it, so no page or script gets round it; acceptance is audited (consent_accepted) and DECLINE signs the user out. Client-certificate and OpenStare access are asked once a day; API tokens (programs) are not asked. The banner is off by default: the site turns it on with the Standard Mandatory DoD Notice and Consent Banner text.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/consent.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (layer); ui/src/auth/WarnGate.tsx; docs/guides/admin.md#notice-and-consent

*Site:* Turn on the warning banner with the DoD standard text [[SITE: banner text]].

### AC-11 Device Lock

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack ends a browser session after 15 minutes without user activity (10 for admins), counted from the user's own input rather than page polling, and the user must sign in again to continue; users can also sign out at any time. The workstation's own screen lock is the site's.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/sessions.rs; ui/src/auth/activity.ts; ui/src/auth/AuthProvider.tsx; docs/guides/admin.md#session-limits

*Site:* Enforce the workstation device lock at [[SITE: time period]] through the endpoint configuration.

### AC-11(1) Pattern-hiding Displays

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

When a session ends, the next call from the page (the pages poll the API) gets 401 and the browser replaces the display with the sign-in page, so no track data stays visible. Pattern-hiding on the workstation lock is the site's.

*Evidence:* ui/src/auth/AuthProvider.tsx; ui/src/auth/Root.tsx

*Site:* Configure workstation lock screens to conceal the display.

### AC-12 Session Termination

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Sessions are kept on the server and end automatically when idle (15 minutes, 10 for admins) and after 24 hours regardless of use; a fourth concurrent sign-in ends the oldest of the account's 3 sessions. Sessions also end at sign-out, password change, account disable and Sign out everywhere, and each end is audited with its reason. The session cookie is a browser-session cookie, so closing the browser drops it; signing out shows an explicit signed-out message.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/maintenance.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/sessions.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#session-limits

### AC-14 Permitted Actions Without Identification or Authentication

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Without identification, OpenTrack answers only GET /healthz (liveness), the sign-in page and its calls (/auth/login, /auth/logout, /auth/public, SAML login, ACS and metadata) and the classification and warning banner text; every other route needs a signed-in account. OT_AUTH=off, which would make every caller an admin, is excluded from accredited deployments by the hardening checklist (F-4).

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/policy.rs (PUBLIC); crates/ot-server/src/control.rs; docs/security/hardening.md; docs/security/stig-mapping.md (F-4)

*Site:* Document these actions in the SSP and never set OT_AUTH=off.

### AC-17 Remote Access

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack is used only over the network (web UI and API), and every remote session is authenticated, role-checked and audited as in AC-2, AC-3 and AU-2; the listeners support TLS and mutual TLS. The [[SITE: organisation]] establishes usage restrictions and authorises remote access to [[SITE: system name]] and its network.

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#sign-in; docs/guides/admin.md#encryption-in-transit; docs/security/hardening.md

*Site:* Document and authorise each type of remote access.

### AC-17(1) Monitoring and Control

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Every sign-in, sign-out and session end (with client address) and every change is recorded in the audit record and exported over OpenTelemetry for monitoring; admins see and end any account's sessions. Monitoring of network-level remote access is the site's.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-store/src/audit.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#audit-record; docs/guides/admin.md#session-limits

*Site:* Monitor remote access in the SIEM and at the boundary.

### AC-17(2) Protection of Confidentiality and Integrity Using Encryption

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

The UI and API are served over TLS with FIPS-approved suites only (OT_TLS_CERT/OT_TLS_KEY, or a TLS proxy with OT_PUBLIC_TLS=1), with HSTS and Secure cookies; TLS is also available for NATS, Redis, feeds, TAK and OpenTelemetry. TLS is not forced by the product: the site must configure it as the hardening checklist requires.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/https.rs; crates/ot-server/src/fips.rs; docs/security/fips.md; docs/security/hardening.md (Encrypt every link); docs/guides/admin.md#web-protections

*Site:* Configure TLS on every link per the hardening checklist and issue the certificates.

### AC-17(3) Managed Access Control Points

### AC-17(4) Privileged Commands and Access

## AU: Audit and Accountability

### AU-1 Policy and Procedures

**Status:** Inherited · **Responsibility:** Site

The [[SITE: organisation]] develops, documents and disseminates the audit and accountability policy and procedures for [[SITE: system name]] and reviews them [[SITE: frequency]]. OpenTrack supplies the product-specific procedures the site can reference: the administrator guide (docs/guides/admin.md), the deployment hardening checklist (docs/security/hardening.md) and the control mapping (docs/security/stig-mapping.md).

*Site:* Write and approve the audit policy and procedures and set their review frequency.

### AU-2 Event Logging

**Status:** Partially implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack records in its audit record: sign-in success and failure (reason, method, address), sign-out, lockout, unlock, session end and idle timeout, password change and expiry, account disable, every decision (account, token, sign-in, source, schema, settings, plugin and track management changes), audit export and chain verification. Not recorded: requests refused for lack of a role (403) or of a session (401), and authentication of individual calls by API token, client certificate or OpenStare session (these create no session; only TLS-level client certificate refusals and OpenStare check failures reach the log, not the audit record). The [[SITE: organisation]] selects the events to log, coordinates with other entities and reviews the selection [[SITE: frequency]].

### IA-2 Identification and Authentication (Organizational Users)

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Every user is identified by a unique account (email) and authenticated by one of: local password, SAML 2.0 through the site's identity provider, a PKI client certificate mapped by CN to an account, an OpenStare session or token (when OpenStare trust is on), or an API token acting as its account. Every change is attributed to that account in the audit record. OT_AUTH=off, which disables sign-in, is excluded from accredited deployments (F-4).

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (identify, layer); crates/ot-server/src/auth/api.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/saml.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/openstare.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#sign-in; docs/security/stig-mapping.md (IA-2, F-4)

*Site:* Issue individual accounts; do not share accounts; never set OT_AUTH=off.

### IA-2(1) Multi-factor Authentication to Privileged Accounts

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

For privileged accounts: OpenTrack has no second factor of its own for local password accounts; multi-factor authentication comes from the site's identity provider (SAML) or a PKI client certificate on a CAC/PIV token, and password sign-in can be turned off once one of SAML or OpenStare sign-in is on. The site enforces MFA at the identity provider, turns Password sign-in off, and keeps password-only local accounts to sealed break-glass accounts used when single sign-on fails.

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#password-sign-in; docs/guides/admin.md#saml; docs/guides/admin.md#client-certificates; docs/security/hardening.md (Sign-in)

*Site:* Enforce MFA at the identity provider or with CAC/PIV client certificates; turn Password sign-in off; document break-glass accounts.

### IA-2(2) Multi-factor Authentication to Non-privileged Accounts

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

For non-privileged accounts: OpenTrack has no second factor of its own for local password accounts; multi-factor authentication comes from the site's identity provider (SAML) or a PKI client certificate on a CAC/PIV token, and password sign-in can be turned off once one of SAML or OpenStare sign-in is on. The site enforces MFA at the identity provider, turns Password sign-in off, and keeps password-only local accounts to sealed break-glass accounts used when single sign-on fails.

*Evidence:* docs/guides/admin.md#password-sign-in; docs/guides/admin.md#saml; docs/guides/admin.md#client-certificates; docs/security/hardening.md (Sign-in)

*Site:* Enforce MFA at the identity provider or with CAC/PIV client certificates; turn Password sign-in off.

### IA-2(8) Access to Accounts — Replay Resistant

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

Authentication runs over TLS, SAML assertions are accepted once and only in answer to a request OpenTrack made in the last 5 minutes, and sessions are signed tokens with a unique id backed by a server-side session record, sent in HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict, Secure cookies. The site configures TLS on the UI and API as the hardening checklist requires.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/saml.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/sessions.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#web-protections; docs/security/hardening.md

*Site:* Serve OpenTrack only over TLS.

### IA-2(12) Acceptance of PIV Credentials

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

With OT_TLS_CLIENT_CA set, OpenTrack accepts PIV/CAC client certificates over mutual TLS, validates the path to the configured CA, checks revocation against the CAs' CRLs (OT_TLS_CLIENT_CRL; unknown status or a stale list fails closed; reloaded on change) and maps the certificate CN to an account. OCSP is not supported (see IA-5(2)). PIV can also be verified by the site's identity provider for SAML sign-on.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/https.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (from_cert); docs/guides/admin.md#client-certificates; docs/security/stig-mapping.md (IA-2(12), F-6)

*Site:* Configure OT_TLS_CLIENT_CA with the DoD CAs, refresh CRLs daily, and map certificate CNs to accounts.

### IA-3 Device Identification and Authentication

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack authenticates the systems it connects to by TLS server certificates and can present client certificates for mutual TLS to NATS, Redis, feeds, TAK Servers and the OpenTelemetry collector; machines connecting to its API authenticate by client certificate (mapped by CN to an account) or API token, and listening TAK outputs can require a client CA. *Site:* Issue device certificates and enable mutual TLS on each link; do not use insecure_skip_verify (F-4).

### IA-4 Identifier Management

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack identifies each account by a unique email, stored case-insensitively, and each API token by name and id with the account it acts as and who made it. Authorising identifiers, assigning them to individuals and preventing reuse of identifiers for [[SITE: time period]] are site processes; deleted accounts remain named in the decision log and audit record.

### IA-5 Authenticator Management

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack enforces the password policy on every password it accepts, including the first admin's, makes admin-set and initial passwords temporary (changed at next sign-in, nothing else allowed until then), stores passwords only as PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hashes (600,000 iterations, FIPS module), writes session.key and initial-admin.txt readable by their owner only, and gives API tokens an expiry and revocation. Resetting a password ends the account's sessions and API tokens. Initial distribution, lost or compromised authenticators and the site's PKI are the site's.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/password.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs; crates/ot-server/src/fips.rs (PBKDF2_ITERATIONS); crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (bootstrap_admin); docs/guides/admin.md#passwords; docs/guides/admin.md#password-policy

*Site:* Distribute initial passwords securely, delete initial-admin.txt, set OT_SESSION_SECRET from a secret store, and rotate API tokens.

### IA-5(1) Password-based Authentication

**Status:** Partially implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Passwords must be at least 15 characters with upper case, lower case, a digit and a special character, differ from the current one by at least 8 characters, not repeat the last 5, and are changed at most once per 24 hours (admin resets exempt) and at least every 60 days; temporary passwords must be changed at first use; values are fixed. Passwords are stored as salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hashes (600,000 iterations) and sent only in the TLS-protected sign-in call. OpenTrack does not check new passwords against a list of commonly used, expected or compromised passwords, as IA-5(1)(a)-(b) requires.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/password.rs (check, check_change); crates/ot-server/src/auth/stig.rs; crates/ot-server/src/fips.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/api.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#password-policy

*Site:* POA&M: add a compromised/common password list check; meanwhile prefer SSO and keep local accounts to break-glass and service use.

### IA-5(2) Public Key-based Authentication

**Status:** Partially implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

For client certificates OpenTrack validates the certification path to the CA in OT_TLS_CLIENT_CA, checks end-entity revocation against CRLs (failing closed on unknown status or a list past its next update, reloading on change), and maps the certificate's CN to an account; the private key stays on the user's token. Revocation status comes from CRLs only: OCSP is not supported (roadmap #21).

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/https.rs; crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs (from_cert); docs/guides/admin.md#client-certificates; docs/security/stig-mapping.md (IA-5(2), F-6)

*Site:* Refresh CRLs at least daily into OT_TLS_CLIENT_CRL; POA&M: OCSP support (roadmap #21).

### IA-5(6) Protection of Authenticators

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** Shared

OpenTrack stores password hashes (never passwords), protects its session signing key (session.key, owner-only, or OT_SESSION_SECRET from the environment), shows new API tokens once and masks source credentials for non-admins. The site protects the /data volume, backups and secret store at the level of the data's classification.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/mod.rs; crates/ot-server/src/fips.rs; docs/guides/admin.md#security; docs/security/hardening.md (Before first start); docs/security/stig-mapping.md (F-2)

*Site:* Encrypt and restrict /data and backups; supply OT_SESSION_SECRET from a secret store.

### IA-6 Authentication Feedback

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

Password fields are masked, and every refused password sign-in gives the same answer (wrong email or password) and takes as long as a wrong password, whether the account is unknown, turned off, locked or the password is wrong; the reason goes only to the audit record. A refused SAML sign-on shows only Single sign-on failed.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/auth/api.rs (login); ui/src/auth/LoginPage.tsx; docs/guides/admin.md#account-lockout

### IA-7 Cryptographic Module Authentication

**Status:** Implemented · **Responsibility:** OpenTrack

All authentication cryptography runs in FIPS 140-3 validated modules: TLS, session and token signatures, password hashing and random secrets in the AWS-LC FIPS module (the process stops if it is not in FIPS mode), and SAML signatures through the OpenSSL 3.0.9 FIPS provider in the image; ring is banned in CI.

*Evidence:* crates/ot-server/src/fips.rs; docs/security/fips.md; deny.toml

*Site:* Run the released image, which carries the OpenSSL FIPS provider.
